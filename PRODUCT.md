# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Young adults in Taiwan (roughly 20 to 35) who rent, ride a scooter, keep a cat or carry a laptop everywhere, and who have
never trusted insurance because it hides how it works. They come to Cover on a phone, often at the moment something just
went wrong (a cracked screen, a scratched scooter, a sick pet), wanting to know exactly what happens next and when the
money arrives. A second audience reads the same site closely: hackathon judges and their own AI agents, checking that
every claim about money is true.

## Product Purpose

Cover is insurance where every dollar is accounted for in public: a price shown as its parts, claims decided by numbered
clauses, PayPal moving the money in seconds, and what the claims pool doesn't need given to a cause. Success is a person
who files a claim and watches it get decided, check by check, and paid before they put the phone down; and a judge who
can trace any number on the page back to the ledger.

## Brand Personality

Precise, transparent, engineered. Cover speaks like the spec sheet of a well-made instrument: exact numbers, named parts,
no adjectives doing the work of facts. Calm under pressure. The emotional goal is earned trust: "I can see how this works."

## Anti-references

- Lemonade's pink, cartoon-cute insurance (Cover must not look like an imitation of it).
- Generic SaaS templates: gradient heroes, identical icon-card grids, a row of big-number stat tiles, pill buttons everywhere.
- Traditional insurers' sites: blue handshake stock photos, family-on-a-beach imagery, walls of fine print.

## Design Principles

1. Show the mechanism. Every promise is shown working (the split of a premium, a claim's checks, the payout's PayPal id), not described.
2. Numbers are the hero. Prices, seconds, clause ids and ledger totals get the strongest typography on the page.
3. Name every part. Clauses, checks and payments carry their ids, like parts on an engineering drawing.
4. Nothing decorative that isn't data. Ornament comes from measurement: rules, ticks, dimension lines, the ledger itself.
5. The same instrument everywhere. The person's claim page, the adjuster's desk and the back office share one visual system.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: body text at least 4.5:1, focus always visible, everything keyboard reachable. Chinese and English throughout.
Every animation has a prefers-reduced-motion alternative, and no content depends on an animation finishing to be visible.
