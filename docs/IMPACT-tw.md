# Cover: potential impact (Taiwan version, kept for reference)

> Superseded by `IMPACT.md`, which makes the case for the US. This is the earlier Taiwan research, kept as written.

Research note for the "Potential Impact" criterion of the PayPal AI Hackathon. Every figure below was checked
against the source linked next to it, on 2026-11-04. Where the only source we could reach was a news report of an
official statistic, the table says so. Figures we could not verify are listed separately in the last section and are
not used in the argument.

## The problem, numbers first

Taiwan has 14.67 million registered scooters, and 1.31 million of them (about 9%) had no compulsory insurance at the
end of 2025. Only about three in ten riders add optional third-party cover, and for light scooters the 2024 figure was
14%. Of 9.64 million homes, only 39.93% carry even the basic residential fire and earthquake policy, and no public
statistic counts renters who insure their own belongings or their liability to a landlord. Meanwhile renting is
rising. The household survey puts renting at 7.62% of households in 2025, up for three years running, while the
central rent subsidy approved 910,000 households in 2025 alone, and over 60% of approved households were aged 18 to 39.
Cats now outnumber dogs, at 1.74 million against 1.46 million, but pet insurance wrote only 32,855 policies in the
first five months of 2026. Most young people in Taiwan who rent, ride and keep pets are uninsured for the things they
actually lose. When people do claim, insurance is where Taiwan's financial disputes concentrate: 68% of all financial
complaints and 79% of formal disputes in the first three quarters of 2025, and more than 80% of non-life complaints in
Q3 2025 were about claims. Where a policy sets no deadline, the law gives an insurer 15 days to pay once the paperwork is
complete; Lemonade reports that roughly 55% of its claims are now automated end to end.

## Who Cover serves

**Wei-Ting, 27, an engineer in Xinzhuang.** She is one of the 910,000 households on the central rent subsidy, which
covers about 40% of her rent. She rides a 125cc scooter to work, has the compulsory policy because the dealer sold it
with the bike, and like seven in ten riders has no optional third-party cover. She has a cat, one of the 1.74 million
in Taiwanese homes, registered because the law now requires it, uninsured like nearly every other. Her landlord's
fire policy, if there is one, covers the building and not her laptop or her liability if her space heater starts a
fire. She has never bought insurance on her own, because every product she looked at asked for a salesperson's
visit, ran to pages of terms, and gave no sign of what happens at claim time.

Each fact in that sketch is a row in the tables above. The audience is narrow on purpose: people roughly 20 to 35 who
rent, ride and keep pets, whose possessions are worth tens of thousands of NT dollars rather than millions, and whose
losses are small, frequent and easy to document (a stolen phone, a scraped scooter, a vet bill). Those are exactly the
claims a rules engine can decide in seconds.

## Honest limits

- **Not a licensed insurer.** Cover sells no real insurance. It runs on PayPal's sandbox, and the charities are
  sandbox accounts standing in for real organisations.
- **Prices are in US dollars** because the sandbox runs in USD. A real Taiwanese product would be priced in NT
  dollars and filed with the regulator.
- **Risk data is rough or synthetic.** District risk levels (flood, earthquake, typhoon, theft) are a hand-built
  first table from public sources in outline (`api/src/zones.js`). Cover's own models are trained on synthetic data
  (`ml/README.md`) and show only that they learned Cover's rules, not that they price real risk.
- **The price tables are not actuarial.** Base rates and factors were set for the demo, not fitted to Taiwanese
  loss data.
- **Instant payment needs fraud control.** Lemonade, with years of data, automates about 55% of claims and its loss
  ratio was 85% to 90% from 2021 to 2023. Cover's caps are deliberately low for that reason.
- **The giveback depends on the claims pool.** In a bad catastrophe year there may be nothing to give, and Lemonade
  itself treats its Giveback as discretionary.
- **Catastrophe exposure.** Taiwan's earthquakes and typhoons concentrate losses the way California's fires did for
  Lemonade. A real Cover needs reinsurance and would build on the existing government-backed residential earthquake scheme rather than replace it.

## Realistic path to market

1. **Start as a broker, not an insurer.** A broker company needs NT$20 million of capital (保險經紀人管理規則 §16),
   one hundredth of the NT$2 billion an insurer needs. As a broker, Cover acts for the insured (保險法 §9), keeps the
   customer experience and the claims front end, and places the risk with a licensed non-life insurer that holds
   the policies and the reserves. An agent licence (保險法 §8) is the alternative if one insurer wants Cover as its
   digital channel.
2. **Test the parts the law doesn't yet allow in the sandbox.** Instant clause-based settlement, a giveback from the
   claims pool, and an outside AI agent buying insurance for a person are new. 金融科技發展與創新實驗條例 allows a
   one-year experiment, extendable by six months, or up to three years if a law has to change (§9).
3. **Only then consider a digital insurer licence.** A non-life digital insurer needs NT$500 million (§29-3 of
   保險業設立許可及管理辦法), and makes sense only once the loss data from the broker years shows the model works in
   Taiwan.
4. **Payments.** PayPal moves the money in the demo. In Taiwan, a live version would also need local payment
   methods and would have to meet the insurer's and the regulator's rules on where premiums are held.

## What we could not verify

- **Renters' insurance take-up.** No government, regulator or industry statistic on how many tenants insure their
  belongings or their liability. Insurer marketing pages exist; we used none of them.
- **Young adults who rent, by age.** The 2020 census and the household survey break tenure down by age of the head of
  household, but we could not reach the tables. We use the rent-subsidy age split instead.
- **Average claim settlement days** for the industry. Insurers disclose late-payment counts and 評議中心 handling
  times, but we found no industry-wide average.
- **Trust in insurers among young people in Taiwan.** We found no recent survey split by age. The only Taiwan survey
  of trust we found dates from 2005 and is too old to use.
- **Optional scooter cover among young riders.** Only all-rider figures exist publicly.
- **The scooter and pet insurance statistics** come from news reports of industry data; we could not reach the
  underlying 保發中心 or 產險公會 tables.
- **The full-year 2025 評議中心 figures**, which were due in February 2026, could not be found; we use Q1 to Q3 2025.

- **The source article's other figures** (1 million customers in 2022, US$94 million revenue, the US$1,461 Forbes
